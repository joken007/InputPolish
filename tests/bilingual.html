Small Tools, Long Lives: Why Focused Software Survives

This page exists to test the InputPolish in-page translator. Every paragraph, list item, table cell, figure caption and definition below is normal English text, so the whole page is a good target for translation.

1. The case for narrow scope

Most software companies chase scale. They raise money, hire aggressively, and optimize for growth above all else. Yet a growing number of developers are discovering that small, focused tools can be both profitable and sustainable.

The economics are straightforward. A tool that solves one problem well can be sold for twenty or thirty dollars a year to a few thousand people. With modest infrastructure costs, that revenue is enough to support one or two people full time, and the author keeps control over the roadmap.

1.1 What a small product can skip

  • A sales team is unnecessary when the product is sold directly to the people who use it.
  • Enterprise procurement reviews, security questionnaires and custom contracts can be skipped entirely.
  • Quarterly growth targets stop dictating which features get built first.

1.2 What it still needs

  1. Fast, boring reliability: the tool must work every morning without drama.
  2. Clear documentation written for the person who will actually use the feature.
  3. A way to say no to requests that would make the core experience worse.

3. Vocabulary often used in these discussions

Burn rate
The speed at which a company spends its available cash before it becomes profitable.
Runway
How many months the business can keep operating at the current spending level.
Churn
The share of customers who stop paying during a given period, usually a month or a year.

4. A note on the tradeoffs

Running a small business is not without friction. Payment processing, customer support, documentation and security updates all demand attention. The author must become a generalist.

There is also the problem of discovery. Without a marketing budget, distribution depends on reputation, word of mouth and a well maintained presence in the communities that the tool serves.
